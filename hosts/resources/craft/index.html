The working craft.

Not every useful idea has a famous name attached. This page is the drawer of ordinary techniques — the mechanics that show up in every tradition of group conversation because they're simply what works. Collected, not invented.

  • Silence is doing work. The urge to rescue a quiet moment is nearly universal and nearly always wrong. People need time to decide whether to say the true thing, and the Host who can sit through ten seconds of quiet gives it to them. If you must do something, count.
  • Invite the unheard without cold-calling. "I'd like to hear from some folks we haven't heard from yet" opens a door without shoving anyone through it. Naming a person and asking them to speak does the opposite — it converts an invitation into a performance request, which is exactly what a quiet person came here to avoid.
  • The over-talker usually doesn't know. Most people who dominate a conversation aren't performing; they're just faster to the gap. A general word to the room beats a public correction, and a quiet word afterward beats both. Save the blunt instruments — going around the table in order — for the rare morning that actually needs one.
  • Side conversations merge on their own. A table that splits in two usually re-merges within a minute if you wait. When it doesn't, invite the livelier thread to the whole table — curiosity works better than a gavel.
