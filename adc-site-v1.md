Every Wednesday morning, a group of us sits down for one of the most interesting conversations we'll have all week.

We talk about Identity, Purpose, and Meaning — not as abstractions, and not as they apply to our businesses or our causes, but as those concepts relate to our own lives. The people actually in the room. The things within our reach.

## Section 2 — The Guiding Principles

*(SETTLED — verbatim from our draft. This is the centerpiece; design the page around it.)*

**A Decent Conversation is a weekly, in-person conversation about Identity, Purpose, and Meaning — as those concepts relate to our own lives, not our businesses or our causes.** These are the principles that keep it that.

**1. Keep it close to the heart.**
We center each conversation on the people in the room and the things within our reach — relationally and interpersonally. Not the news, not the nation, not abstractions or causes. This is the one guideline that does the most work; it's why this is a conversation we don't have any other time of the week.

**2. Stories before opinions.**
We follow personal history and lived experience — how someone got to where they are — rather than takes on external issues. We ask about the life, not the position.

**3. Low ego, guard down.**
Trust is the precondition for everything else. We come willing to be a little vulnerable, slow to signal, with no concern for being the smartest person in the room.

**4. What's learned here leaves here; what's said here stays here.**
The lessons travel out into our lives. The contents stay in the room. This is the promise that makes the rest possible.

**5. One conversation, fully present.**
One conversation at a time, phones away, real listening. We try to ask a question based on what someone just said — and to leave room rather than fill it.
